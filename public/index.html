      How to Play Hnefatafl
      
                
            
      
            
      
                
      Pronunciation & Origin
      
                
      Hnefatafl is pronounced "nef-ah-tah-ful" (roughly). It's an ancient Norse board game, also known as "King's Table" or "Viking Chess."
      
                
                
      Historical Context
      
                
      Hnefatafl was played throughout medieval Scandinavia and the broader Viking world. The original rules have been lost to time, so modern versions are reconstructions based on archaeological evidence, board layouts found in ancient sites, and references in medieval literature.
      
                
                
      Game Overview
      
                
      This is an asymmetric strategy game with two sides:
      
                
        
                    
      • Defenders (D) and the King (K) - fewer pieces, start in the center
        • 
                    
      • Attackers (A) - more numerous, start around the edges
        • 
                
      
                
                
      Objective
      
                
        
                    
      • Defenders win if the King reaches a corner square or if the King reaches an edge square while surrounded by a 'fort' of uncapturable defenders and is still free to move
        • 
                    
      • Attackers win if they capture the King or completely surround all defenders
        • 
                
      
                
                
      Basic Rules
      
                
        
                    
      • Movement: All pieces move like a rook in chess (horizontally or vertically any number of squares)
        • 
                    
      • Captures: Pieces are captured by being sandwiched between two enemy pieces, and only occur as a result of an enemy move. Captures are mandatory and automatic.
        • 
                    
      • Special Squares:
                        
          
                            
        • Throne (center, yellow): Only the King can occupy this square. When not occupied, the throne is hostile to attackers and the King, and can be used to capture
          • 
                            
        • Corners (red): Act as "hostile" squares for captures, only the King can enter
          • 
                        
        
                    
        • 
                    
      • King Capture: The King must be surrounded on all four sides to be captured (or three sides if against the throne)
        • 
                
      
                
                
      Forts
      
                
      Defenders can form protective formations called "forts" where they cannot be captured if properly arranged on the board's edge.
      
                
                
      How to Use This Interface
